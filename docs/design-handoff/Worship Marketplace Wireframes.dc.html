Red italics = behavior rule the screen has to enforce.
Blue italics = decision I need from you before this can be built.
$ Vertical red bar = monetization opportunity (spec'd or newly proposed).
Form factor: musician flows are drawn at phone width, church + admin flows at desktop width — musicians live in the Facebook group on their phones; churches post from an office computer. Both are responsive in reality; this split just shows each at its likely-primary size.
Two principles held constant across every screen
1. Pay types are visually equal
Paid / Volunteer render as the same neutral chip, same weight, same order everywhere. No color coding (no green = paid), no icons, no "Paid" first, no count badges implying one is more common or desirable. There is no sort-by-pay control anywhere in the UI — not disabled, not present.
2. No theology, denomination, or affiliation anywhere
No field, no filter, no tag, no facet. A church may mention it in free-text description only. Style tags (contemporary, gospel, liturgical, traditional…) are musical style, and the wireframes keep them labeled "Musical style" to avoid drift into denominational shorthand.
Decision: "liturgical" and "gospel" are musical styles that in practice correlate with tradition. Keep them, or trim the list to purely musical descriptors (e.g. hymns, contemporary, acoustic)?

2jMonetization map — where money could sit, and what it would cost youPHASE 2+
Already in the spec
Boost — best placements: after posting (2c step 04) and on a zero-applicant slot (2f step 04). Sell it as a fix for a felt problem. Risk: too many featured cards and the top of the feed stops being trustworthy — cap per filtered view.
Post cap → paid unlimited — the counter is visible from day one as a neutral fact (1d). Don't monetize it until a real church actually hits 4/month; before that it reads as an invented wall.
Local sponsor placements — one card after result 5 in the feed (2a), plus the digest email. Music stores, lesson instructors, sound techs. Never above organic results, always labeled.
Tip jar — footer from day one, one line, "Help keep this platform free."
New ideas worth considering
Sponsored weekly digest — "12 new services in North Texas this week." Owns the retention loop and is the single most sellable inch of real estate you'll have. Build it for retention; monetize it second.
Saved-search alerts as the priority-timing product — free = weekly digest, paid = instant. Build the timing switch now (2a step 03) even if everything's free at launch.
Church-side "urgent fill" badge — the one-time service inside 72 hours is where churches feel real pain. A time-boxed urgency boost is easier to justify than a generic one.
Vendor directory (not ads) — a paid listing page for sound techs, AV installers, and instrument techs. Adjacent to your audience, doesn't touch the musician↔church loop, and can't cannibalize the free core.
Regional expansion sponsorships — when you open region #2, a local music retailer sponsors the launch. Sells the audience, not access.
The one thing I'd protect absolutely: never charge for, gate, or rank by pay type. Any monetization that makes a paid service more visible than a volunteer one breaks the equality principle the whole platform is built on.
